In 2023, a small group of us set out to answer a simple question: what actually goes wrong when you put large language models into real software?
At the time, there was a lot of excitement but not much structure. The [OWASP Top 10 for Large Language Model Applications](https://www.linkedin.com/redir/redirect?url=https%3A%2F%2Fgenai%2Eowasp%2Eorg%2Fllm-top-10%2F&urlhash=MHvY&trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block) was our attempt to bring some clarity to that moment. It gave practitioners a shared vocabulary and, just as important, gave builders something concrete to defend against.
Since then, things have not exactly stood still.
That original effort grew into the broader [OWASP Generative AI Security Project](https://www.linkedin.com/redir/redirect?url=https%3A%2F%2Fgenai%2Eowasp%2Eorg%2F&urlhash=Igly&trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block). What started as a single list is now a community producing research, guidance, and practical material across a wide range of GenAI topics. Most recently, we introduced the [OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications](https://www.linkedin.com/redir/redirect?url=https%3A%2F%2Fgenai%2Eowasp%2Eorg%2Fresource%2Fowasp-top-10-for-agentic-applications-for-2026%2F&urlhash=A4Vu&trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block) to address systems that are no longer just answering prompts, but taking actions, making decisions, and interacting with other systems on our behalf.
Which brings us to today. It is time to revisit the core LLM Top 10 list.
We are officially kicking off the 2026 update to the OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications with a community questionnaire to gather input from practitioners. The survey is designed to guide you in providing feedback in a structured way, and we will also ask whether you are interested in actively volunteering or working more directly with us as part of the update process.

A quick note on access: the survey requires a Google login. That adds a bit of friction, but anonymous surveys are far too easy to manipulate, especially in an era when everyone has automation at their fingertips. We want input from real practitioners. OWASP members can use their OWASP-issued email, but any Google ID will work.
The survey will be open for just one week, so please get your input in as soon as you can. We are intentionally keeping the window short to keep momentum on the update process.
[ Ads Dawson ](https://www.linkedin.com/in/adamdawson0?trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-mention) and I will continue to run this effort within the OWASP Generative AI Security Project, working alongside a global set of contributors who care deeply about getting this right. As before, this is an open, collaborative process. Drafts will be discussed publicly. Assumptions will be challenged. And we will rely on the community to keep us honest.
If you would like to learn more about the project or contribute more deeply, visit [https://genai.owasp.org/contributing/](https://www.linkedin.com/redir/redirect?url=https%3A%2F%2Fgenai%2Eowasp%2Eorg%2Fcontributing%2F&urlhash=F-zp&trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block).
Let’s build the next version together.

Amazing - looking forward to the update!
💡 1. Why consider a Top 15 or more Increasing complexity of LLM systems: Since 2023, LLMs are no longer isolated—they are integrated into real applications and interact with other systems. New types of risks: - Unanticipated agentic behaviors - Exploitation through complex prompt chains - Leaks of contextual data or connected APIs - Manipulation via autonomous or multi-modal agents Limitations of the Top 10: Some emerging risks no longer fit into a concise list of 10. A Top 15 would allow inclusion of critical vectors that might otherwise be overlooked. 💡 2. Points to consider Readability vs. comprehensiveness: The more items in the list, the harder it is to remember or prioritize. Possible categorization: If moving to a Top 15 or Top 20, it may be useful to group risks by type: security, privacy, robustness, governance, ethics. Community adoption: The OWASP Top 10 is a widely recognized standard; changing the number could require clear communication to explain the decision.
